# The problem

## The call

An adult child, two states away, gets a call: Mum has a cardiology follow-up on
Thursday at 10:40. She no longer drives.

What follows is not a medical problem. It is a logistics problem that no single
system owns:

- Call the clinic to confirm the slot and how long it will run.
- Call a non-emergency medical transport company. Explain the walker, the
  wheelchair, the fact that she cannot manage stairs unassisted.
- Book the return leg without knowing when the appointment will actually end.
- On Thursday morning, wait. Was she collected? Did the driver arrive at all?
  Did she get inside the building? Call her mobile; she does not hear it in her
  bag. Call the transport company's dispatch line and hold.
- Pay three separate parties, none of whom talk to each other.

## Why it is hard

The failure is **informational**, not clinical. Every party holds one fragment
of the truth and none of them holds the whole picture:

| Party | Knows | Does not know |
| ----- | ----- | ------------- |
| Clinic | The slot, roughly how long | Whether the patient is on the way |
| Transport provider | Which driver, which vehicle | When the appointment will end |
| Driver | Where the car is, right now | Anything about the appointment |
| Family | That an appointment exists | Everything else, on the day |

The distress that drives churn in this market is not a missed appointment. It
is **the two hours of silence in the middle of a workday** — the interval
between "the car should have arrived by now" and someone finally picking up a
phone.

## Who feels it

The family member feels the anxiety and pays to remove it. The patient feels
the disruption — a stranger at the door with no name attached, a car they were
not told about. The dispatcher feels the phone calls, which are pure cost for
their business. The clinic feels the no-shows.

## What "solved" looks like

A family member opens one app and can answer, without interacting with it:

1. What is next?
2. Is it on track?
3. What do I do if it is not?

Everything else in this product exists to make those three answers true and
current.

## What we are not solving

Not the clinical problem. Not the payer problem. Not the shortage of accessible
vehicles. Coordination is the fragment nobody owns and the one where a small
team can be decisively better than the alternatives — which today are a
telephone and a paper diary.
